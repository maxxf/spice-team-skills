## The draft

We audited 31 emails Spice actually sent to clients over the last four months. Here is what came out of it, and what changes today.

Start with the email that kicked this off. Shai at Westville replied to one of ours last week with this:

> "3. These are VERY AI looking. Is there a way to make that a little more subtle"

A paying client told us our email reads like a machine wrote it. Sit with that for a second before we move on.

Here is what he was reacting to. That email was the longest of the 31. 466 words. The only thing we actually needed back from him, approval to launch a test, sat at item three of four, roughly 300 words down. And it linked a full analysis, then retyped the analysis directly above the link.

That is what AI-shaped writing looks like from the other side of the inbox. Not a vocabulary problem. A shape problem.

That is the pattern across the whole sample. The longer our emails get, the further down the ask slides. Nine emails in the audit ran long. Exactly one of them put the ask in the first three lines.

So, three rules, live now.

**150 words on a composed client email.** Over 200 is a fail. Replies under 40 words are exempt, so "heard, launching Thursday" stays legal forever.

**The ask goes in the first three lines.** Four items to cover? Lead with the one that needs a decision, not the one that happened first. Chronology belongs in the linked doc.

**Link the analysis instead of retyping it.** We already link. 10 of the 13 emails that cited numbers pointed at a source doc. Our problem is what we write after the link.

Proof that it works, and it is ours. Someone on our side sent the same overdue-invoice email twice, two weeks apart, to the same person.

July 16, 110 words:

> Tohm --
>
> I'm reaching out about your outstanding balance with Spice. We currently have three unpaid monthly cycles on the Dayglow/AWAN Tohm Pricing plan. You can pay each one directly at the links below:
>
> • April: $1,653.12 — Pay invoice
> • May: $1,653.12 — Pay invoice
> • June: $1,653.12 — Pay invoice
>
> Total past due: $4,959.36 (oldest invoice now 79 days overdue)
>
> Could you clear these this week, or let us know a firm payment date? Happy to resend anything you need. If we don't hear back, we'll need to pause service on the account until the balance is settled.
>
> Thanks for taking care of this.

July 30, 65 words:

> Hi Tohm,
>
> Three Dayglow/AWAN invoices are still open, going back to April. The total is $4,959.36.
>
> $1,653.12 — April cycle, due Apr 28, now 90 days past due — Pay invoice
> $1,653.12 — May cycle, due May 28, now 60 days past due — Pay invoice
> $1,653.12 — June cycle, due Jun 28, now 29 days past due — Pay invoice
>
> Are you planning to pay these off?

Same information, half the words, and the second one is better in every way that matters. It opens with the fact. It drops "I'm reaching out about." It puts the aging on each line instead of burying it in a parenthetical. It closes on one blunt question instead of a question plus two hedges. Nobody wrote a rule to make that happen. Somebody just wrote the thing twice and the second try was sharper. (Copy the structure, not the dashes. Those are on the voice guide kill list.)

Now the part I actually care about, because this is exactly where a word limit goes wrong.

Short is not permission to be curt.

Every client email carries at least one line that only a person who looked at that account this week could have written. A detail. A callback to something they said on the call. Something odd in their numbers that you noticed and cannot fully explain yet. That line is not optional and it does not count against you.

A short email with nothing specific in it does not read as respect for someone's time. It reads as disengaged, like the account is on autopilot and you are clearing a queue. 150 words plus one real observation says "I know this account well enough to be quick." 150 words with nothing in it says "I have not thought about you since the last one of these." Same length, opposite message.

If you cannot write that line, you are not ready to send the email. Go look at the account first.

There is no skill to remember here. The standard applies to client comms on its own, whether you ask for it or not. Write a client an email in Cowork and it comes out shaped this way. Paste one you already wrote and it gets checked before it goes anywhere.

You can also just ask for the draft. One sentence ("recap for Fresh Kitchen on the Olipop launch") and it writes the whole thing into your own Gmail drafts, already checked. It pulls the numbers, picks the format, and tells you what it stripped and why. It never sends. You send.

To get it, paste `update spice skills` into Cowork. That pulls plugin 1.31.0. `post-client-meeting`, `ratings-reply`, `client-onboarding`, and `email-template-designer` run through the same standard now, so recaps and onboarding emails come out the right shape whether or not you think about it.

The full standard is in `references/client-comms-style.md`: the skeleton, the banned-phrase table, and six before/after pairs pulled from our own threads.
